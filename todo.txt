### 3. Zerodha API Rate Limits & Call Budget

Zerodha enforces strict rate limits across its Kite Connect endpoints:

* **Historical Data Endpoint Limit:** **3 requests per second** max.
* **General API Limits:** **10 requests per second** across non-historical endpoints.
* **How Our Code Complies:** In `data/fetch_zerodha.py`, we request historical data in **60-day chunks** with an explicit `time.sleep(0.4)` delay between calls. This maintains a safe rate of **2.5 requests per second**, preventing rate-limit bans (HTTP 429) during the historical download process.


We **are** downloading and using OHLCV (Open, High, Low, Close, Volume) data—it is fundamental to the entire strategy.

* **The Raw Data Has It:** The raw download from Zerodha contains `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, and `oi` (Open Interest).


* **The Processed File Keeps It:** When `data/process_raw.py` cleans the data into `market_data.csv`, it formats it into `timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume`.


* **Feature Engineering Uses It:** `features/feature_engineer.py` uses High and Low for Parkinson Volatility, Volume for the Volume Z-score, and Close for Log Returns and Fractional Differencing.



### Why Raw OHLC Prices Aren't Fed Directly to the RL Agent

Raw prices (like Close = 10,530 or Open = 10,531) are **non-stationary**—prices constantly drift upward or downward over years. Deep learning models struggle to generalize on raw prices because a Nifty price of 24,000 in 2026 looks completely alien to a model that only saw Nifty at 10,000 in 2018.

To fix this:

* We transform raw OHLC prices into **stationary features** (Log Returns, Fractional Differencing, Parkinson Volatility) so the neural network learns universal market patterns regardless of price level.


* We **keep** the original `close` price and `timestamp` in the final dataset (`featured_market_data.csv`) so the Gymnasium environment (`strict_sim_env.py`) can execute trades, calculate PnL, and track real account balance during simulation.


### 1. Bandwidth & Storage Requirements

* **Continuous 1-Minute Futures Data (2018–2026):**
* **Data Volume:** 8.6 years $\times$ ~250 trading days/year $\times$ 375 minutes/day $\approx$ 800,000 candle rows.
* **Payload Size:** At ~100 bytes per OHLCV row, the raw text payload is approximately **80 MB to 100 MB**.
* **Network Bandwidth:** Negligible. A standard 10 Mbps broadband or mobile connection can download the entire historical dataset in under 30 seconds.


* **Full Historical Options Chain Data (2018–2026):**
* **Data Volume:** Options require tracking ~30 active strike prices per expiry across weekly and monthly contracts.
* **Payload Size:** 800,000 rows $\times$ 30 strikes $\times$ 2 (Calls & Puts) $\approx$ 48,000,000 candle rows, totaling **5 GB to 8 GB** of uncompressed CSV data.
* **Network Bandwidth:** Demands a stable 50+ Mbps connection to avoid HTTP timeout errors during multi-gigabyte chunk requests.

### 2. Capital Efficiency Math: Futures vs. Options (50,000 INR Base)

**The Futures Leverage Constraint**

* **SEBI Margin Requirement:** Standard Nifty 50 Futures require a non-negotiable SpanishSPAN + Exposure margin of approximately **1,10,000 INR to 1,28,000 INR** per lot (75 shares).
* **Capital Gap:** With a 50,000 INR starting balance, opening a raw futures contract is mathematically impossible due to exchange-enforced leverage limits.

**The Hybrid Options Alternative (Synthetic Futures / Delta Exposure)**

* **Deep In-The-Money (ITM) Options:** Buying a Deep ITM Call or Put option with a **Delta ($\Delta$) of 0.85 to 0.90** closely mimics a futures contract's linear price action at a fraction of the cost.
* **Margin Cost:** A 300-point ITM Nifty Call option trading at ~400 INR premium requires a total cash outlay of $400 \times 75 = \mathbf{30,000\text{ INR}}$, well within the 50,000 INR capital allocation limit.
* **Theta ($\Theta$) Decay Penalty:** Option buyers face constant non-linear time decay ($\Theta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial t}$), which actively reduces contract value if the market consolidates horizontally.

---

### 3. Technical Approach: Hybrid Options Execution Architecture

To solve the capital constraint while preventing option chain complexity from distorting model convergence:

1. **Train the Reinforcement Learning Agent on Continuous Futures:**
* The RL policy uses continuous futures state vectors (log-returns, fractional differentiation, Parkinson volatility) to master directional market edge without noise from strike selection, time decay, or IV shifts.


2. **Execute via Dynamic Option Strike Mapping:**
* When the continuous futures agent emits a **Long Signal ($a_t = +1$)**, the execution pipeline automatically fetches the current Nifty spot price $S_t$, queries the live Zerodha option chain, selects the Call Strike $K$ with $\Delta \ge 0.85$, and executes an order for that option premium.
* When the agent emits a **Short Signal ($a_t = -1$)**, the pipeline executes an equivalent Deep ITM Put option.
* When the agent emits a **Flat Signal ($a_t = 0$)**, all open option positions are liquidated to avoid holding overnight theta decay.


3. **Friction Engine Adjustments:**
* The simulation environment (`env/strict_sim_env.py`) evaluates trade friction using option bid-ask spreads (~0.50 to 1.50 INR per lot) and STT (0.125% on premium exercise or sell-side transactions) to maintain zero-lookahead fidelity under real market conditions.
